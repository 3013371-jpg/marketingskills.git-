# Desire → Angle → Structure (Native Ad Architecture)

A working framework for native / advertorial-style ads, rooted in Eugene
Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*. Native ads live or die on matching the
right desire, the right angle into it, and the right structure to carry it — in
that order, top down.

## The Three Layers

```
DESIRE      the mass want the market already feels
  └─ ANGLE      the specific entry point into that desire for one segment
       └─ STRUCTURE   the copy shape that delivers the angle
```

### 1. Desire (the want you tap, never create)

The broad, pre-existing want in the market. You don't manufacture it — you
channel it. One ad serves one desire.

> **Example:** *weight loss.*

### 2. Angle (the specific entry point)

A narrower cut of the desire tied to a specific segment, cause, or situation.
The angle is *why this particular person*, in *their* circumstance, feels the
desire right now. Narrow angles outperform broad ones because the prospect
thinks "this is about me."

> **Example:** within *weight loss* → **hormonal weight gain, specifically
> after pregnancy.** Same desire, a far sharper entry point than "lose weight."

One desire yields many angles (hormonal / post-pregnancy, menopausal, stress
cortisol, thyroid, post-40 metabolism, medication-related…). Each angle is a
distinct ad — and distinct angles reach distinct pockets of the audience, the
same reason hook diversity earns incremental learning (see
[hook-system.md](hook-system.md)).

### 3. Structure (the copy shape)

The structure is *how the argument is built* once you've chosen the angle. The
same angle can be written as several different structures, and the right one
depends mostly on **awareness level** (problem-aware → solution-aware →
product-aware; see the "Audience & Intent" inputs in SKILL.md).

## The Master Diagnostic

For any native ad, run the prospect's journey as a three-beat test:

> **Have I named the need, dramatised it until he feels how badly he needs the
> solution, then presented the product as inevitable?**

1. **Name the need** — put the exact problem/desire into words (in the
   prospect's own language).
2. **Dramatise it** — make the cost of *not* solving it felt, not just stated,
   until the want is urgent.
3. **Present the product as inevitable** — position the product as the obvious,
   almost unavoidable resolution of the dramatised need.

If a native ad skips a beat, it usually underperforms: unnamed need → no
recognition; undramatised need → no urgency; product not made inevitable → no
action.

## The Problem-Solving Ad (and its range)

Schwartz's workhorse structure for problem-aware markets. It is not one shape
but a **range**, from the barest to the fullest:

| Form | Shape | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| Naked problem | Name the problem alone — the problem is so acute that naming it *is* the hook | "Corns?" |
| Problem + solution | Name the problem, then present the product as the resolution | "Corns? Our pads remove them." |
| Problem + solution + limitation removed | Add the mechanism that strips away the objection the prospect expects | "Remove corns — **without surgery**" |

The "…without surgery" move is the highest form: it names the need, implies the
solution, *and* removes the limitation the prospect was bracing for, all at
once. It ties directly to the desire operations in the copywriting skill
(remove an old limitation) — see
[copywriting/references/intensifying-desire.md](../../copywriting/references/intensifying-desire.md).

## A Working Structure Taxonomy

*(This layer is still being refined — treat it as scaffolding to test and
extend, not a closed list. Match structure to awareness.)*

| Structure | Lead with | Best when prospect is | Beats it hits |
|-----------|-----------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Naked problem** | The problem, stated bare | Highly problem-aware, problem acute | Name |
| **Problem → Solution** | The problem, dramatised, then the product | Problem-aware | Name → Dramatise → Inevitable |
| **Problem → Solution → Limitation removed** | As above, plus removing the expected objection | Problem-aware but skeptical / braced for a catch | Name → Dramatise → Inevitable (+ limitation) |
| **Solution-only (pure promise)** | The desired outcome directly, little/no problem build-up | Solution-aware — they already feel the problem | Inevitable (need is pre-felt) |
| **Mechanism / discovery** | The *reason it works* (the unique mechanism or "why") | Solution-aware, comparing options | Inevitable via credibility |
| **Story / advertorial** | A narrative that embeds the problem and its resolution | Any stage; strong for cold/native feeds | All three, delivered implicitly |

**Rule of thumb:** the *more* aware the prospect, the *less* problem
dramatisation you need — lean toward solution-only and mechanism structures.
The *less* aware, the fuller the problem-solving structure must be.

## How to use it when generating native ads

1. **Fix the desire** — one want per ad.
2. **Pick the angle** — the narrowest segment/cause entry point you can defend
   (ground it in reviews/comments per the Grounded Inputs rules).
3. **Choose the structure** from awareness level, not habit — then write it.
4. **Run the master diagnostic** on the draft: named? dramatised? inevitable?
5. **Vary the structure across the batch**, not just the words — different
   structures on the same angle are genuine tests; reworded copy is not.
