# Intensifying Desire — The Seven Operations (Breakthrough Advertising)

Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* defines the job of the headline as
one thing: to seize the desire the prospect already has and intensify it. You
do not create desire — you channel and amplify it.

**Core insight:** The seven ways Schwartz lists for strengthening the desire in
a headline are not just headline tactics. They are the *master blueprint for the
entire landing page*. A great landing page is these seven operations, sequenced
down the page — each section performing one of them on the reader's existing
desire until the desire is strong enough to overcome the friction of acting.

Read the page as a single argument that keeps doing one of these seven jobs,
section after section.

## The Seven Operations

| # | Operation | What it does to the desire |
|---|-----------|---------------------------|
| a | **Reinforce the desire** | Make the prospect want the end result more intensely than when they arrived. |
| b | **Sharpen the image** | Make vivid and concrete *exactly how* the product satisfies the desire. |
| c | **Extend where and when** | Expand the situations, times, and places the product delivers that satisfaction. |
| d | **Introduce new proof** | Add credibility the prospect hasn't seen — the desire feels more attainable. |
| e | **Announce a better mechanism** | Show a mechanism that satisfies the desire *more effectively* than before. |
| f | **Announce a mechanism that removes a limitation** | Show a mechanism that strips away an old constraint that blocked satisfaction. |
| g | **Change the image/mechanism to escape competition** | Reframe so the product no longer competes head-to-head — it's in a category of its own. |

### (a) Reinforce the desire for the product

Restate and amplify the outcome the prospect already wants. Don't introduce a
new want — turn up the volume on the existing one. This is the primary job of
the hero headline and recurs wherever the page restates the payoff.

### (b) Sharpen the image of how it satisfies the desire

Make the satisfaction concrete and sensory. Replace "save time" with the exact
picture: the Monday report that now writes itself while they drink coffee. This
is what benefit sections, screenshots, and "a day in the life" copy do.

### (c) Extend where and when the desire is satisfied

Show the product delivering in more moments than the prospect first imagined —
more use cases, more contexts, more of their week. Use-case blocks, "works with
your stack," and persona sections all perform this operation.

### (d) Introduce new proof

Supply evidence the prospect has not yet encountered: numbers, named results,
testimonials, case studies, logos, demonstrations. New proof makes the desired
outcome feel real and reachable, not just promised.

### (e) Announce a mechanism that does it better

Name the *mechanism* — the specific reason your product satisfies the desire
more effectively. Not "we're better," but *how*: the engine, method, or process
that produces a superior result. Schwartz's "unique mechanism."

### (f) Announce a mechanism that removes an old limitation

Identify the constraint that has kept the prospect from the outcome (setup time,
required expertise, cost, risk) and show the mechanism that removes it.
"Without writing code," "no credit card," "no SEO experience required."

### (g) Change the image or mechanism to escape the competition entirely

When the prospect is comparing you to direct rivals, reframe the product into a
new category or image so the old comparison no longer applies. You stop being
"a cheaper X" and become "the only Y." This is positioning as a desire move.

## Using it to build a landing page

1. **Identify the one dominant desire** the traffic arrives with. Everything on
   the page serves this single desire — do not split it.
2. **Open with (a).** The hero reinforces that desire at full intensity and
   names the outcome in the prospect's own language.
3. **Sequence the middle of the page** as a run of operations (b)–(f), each
   section doing exactly one job: sharpen the image, extend where/when, drop new
   proof, reveal the better mechanism, remove the limitation. One idea per
   section — the same rule the skill applies everywhere.
4. **Use (g) where you face a crowded field.** If the prospect is cross-shopping,
   add a reframe section (comparison, "the category we invented") that lifts you
   out of the direct fight.
5. **Close by reinforcing (a) again** next to the CTA, with risk reversal to
   remove the last limitation (f) on acting now.

**Diagnostic:** For every section on a page, ask *which of the seven is this
doing?* A section that does none of them is not advancing the desire — cut it or
rewrite it until it performs one clearly.
